# Reading, writing, and conversations

[Documentation](README.md) · [Fedistack](../README.md)

- Following, local, federated, bookmarks, hashtag, and server-list timelines, with pagination.
- On mobile, pull down at the top of a feed or connected profile, then release when the spinner is ready to refresh. Short pulls cancel. Existing content stays visible while checking for updates; repeated pulls within five seconds reuse the latest result.
- **Settings → Fedistack** shows the application version, starting at **0.1.0**.
- Links can show a framed thumbnail, title, and site details in the feed and reader. YouTube videos include a thumbnail and an external watch link. Other sites use available preview metadata; some sites block direct lookups. **Settings → Link previews** turns the cards and their loading on or off, with your choice saved on this device. Previews are on by default.
- Tap your bottom-right avatar (or your account in the desktop sidebar) to open **Your profile**. It shows your configured header image, bio, profile fields, post count, followers, and following. **Settings** sits beside **Your writing** below your profile cover and is also in the desktop sidebar.
- On your connected account’s profile, tap your profile picture or background image to choose a replacement from your device. Selecting an image uploads it immediately; cancelling the picker leaves it unchanged.
- Profiles open on **Posts**, which shows top-level posts. **Replies** shows authored replies, and **Boosts** shows shared posts. Your own profile adds **Likes** and **Saved**, in that order, marked with locks and visible only to you. Likes collect posts you enjoyed; Saved keeps posts for later. Individual likes may still be visible to authors and on your server. A compact row above the profile counts shows likes, boosts, and replies on your displayed posts, with colored bars and the number of posts included. The header counts open **Followers** (people who follow the account) and **Following** (people the account follows). Lists and activity have **Load more** controls. Sparse activity scans up to four pages at a time and retains a cursor to continue. Direct messages stay in Conversations.
- Tap an author's name or avatar in a post, comment, search result, recommendation, or follower list to view their profile inside Fedistack. Follow, unfollow, and message them there. An optional **Original profile** link opens their server. Remote follower lists may be incomplete or private.
- Notes show their full text and attachments in the feed. Tap the timestamp to open the conversation. Article previews show a headline, a short subtitle or excerpt, and **Continue reading**. For untitled articles, the preview heading starts after any opening URLs or mentions.
- **Discover** mixes local and federated articles and notes in one continuous feed, with matches for your recent likes, followed hashtags, and lists. It shows up to 20 posts initially and automatically adds more as you approach the bottom. **Load more posts** also works manually. Opening a post and choosing **Back to reading** restores the loaded feed and your scroll position. **Refresh** checks for newer posts while keeping the existing mix in order. Picks favor unseen articles and vary authors and servers; when the available pool is exhausted, the least recently shown articles can return. Discovery uses content known to your connected server.
- **People to follow** appears as a scrollable carousel in Discover, including on phones, and as compact rows in the desktop sidebar. Swipe or use the arrows to browse cards, then choose **See all** for the full list. Follow people directly or use **×** in the carousel or full list to dismiss a suggestion; dismissals are saved in this browser for each account. Suggestions use the server's recommendations when available, with known timeline authors as a fallback. Accounts you follow, have requested to follow, blocked, or muted are excluded.
- **People to follow** appears after the first batch of Discover posts so you can start reading immediately. Local and federated posts share the same feed without separate tabs.
- Discover excludes posts marked as liked by your server, recent favourites, and posts Fedistack has previously observed you liking, even after you unlike them. It remembers liked identifiers and the last 500 article picks separately for each account, in local storage when **Keep me signed in** is enabled and otherwise in this tab's session storage. Clearing that storage removes the local history. Past likes that were undone in another client before Fedistack saw them cannot be recovered from the server's current favourites.
- Each discovery mix scans at most three pages per public timeline, samples up to two lists and three interest hashtags, and refreshes the recent-favourites, followed-hashtag, and list metadata every five minutes or after a related change in Fedistack. It continues older public pages on later mixes while also checking the newest page. Unsupported optional features do not block public reading. Private posts, replies, content warnings, and your own posts are excluded from article recommendations and previews; discovery language preferences apply throughout.
- Open **Settings → Hashtags & lists → Manage hashtags** to follow or unfollow topics. Tap a subscribed hashtag to read its timeline. Subscriptions are saved on your server and bring matching posts into Following when supported by the server.
- The **Lists** tab beside **Following**, **Local**, and **Federated** opens a list picker and **Manage lists**. Choose a list to read its timeline. **Manage lists** (also in **Settings → Hashtags & lists**) lets you create lists, rename them, search people you already follow to add them, and remove members. Lists group accounts; hashtag subscriptions follow topics. Deleting a list asks for confirmation and keeps your account follows. Unsupported server features show an explanation in the management dialog.
- Open **Saved** from **Home** or your profile to read your bookmarked posts. Removing a bookmark in your profile removes it from that collection immediately after the server confirms it.
- Articles/notes filters, following-only filtering, search, topic search, follows and follow requests.
- Timeline loads and **Load older posts** automatically scan up to 3 server pages (40 posts requested per page), aiming for 10 new publications that match the content, language, and following filters. Matches appear as pages arrive, without waiting for optional relationship details unless **Following only** is enabled. Scanning stops at the end of the timeline, on an error, or at the page limit; use **Load older posts** to continue. Changing filters also fills sparse results from older pages. Search uses the server's returned results without this extra scanning.
- Recent feeds are cached separately for each account and timeline in this browser tab. Returning to Home or a full timeline within a minute reuses the saved feed; older snapshots stay visible while fresh posts load. The cache survives a tab reload, expires after a day, and is cleared on logout and changes to posts, follows, filters, or lists. Private feed posts stay in the tab's session storage, and direct messages are excluded. Cached pages remain readable if a refresh fails while offline. Loading older pages does not renew head freshness. Profiles also keep a small in-memory cache per account and activity tab, with the same one-minute freshness and mutation invalidation; follow metadata never delays reading.
- Use **Add language** in **Settings → Discovery languages** to choose one or more languages. On phones, tap your profile avatar, then **Settings**. Tap a language chip to remove it, or **Show all languages** to clear the list. Discover shows posts matching any selected language, including regional variants, using your server's language labels. Unlabeled posts are hidden while filters are selected; load older posts for more results. Your choices are saved on this device, and an older single-language preference carries over. Home and Saved keep all languages.
- Likes, comments preserving the parent visibility, content warnings, and canonical publication sharing. **Repost** shares public or unlisted posts with your followers; its count and highlighted state update after the server confirms it. Click again to undo. Reposting is disabled for followers-only posts and direct messages. Your own comments have a visible **Delete comment** action with confirmation.
- **Share publication** copies a short `#@username/…` link to the current Fedistack site, opening the post in its Markdown reader. The username identifies the author at a glance; the post ID keeps the link working if their username changes. The source server is only included when it differs from the website's origin. UUIDs use a lossless compact encoding without a prefix or redundant leading zero groups; older UUID links with `~` and existing `#read/…` and `#p/…` links still work. Public and unlisted posts can load without signing in, when the server allows public API access. Readers connected to another server can interact after the post resolves there. **Open original post** is a separate link to the author's original publication. Private posts still require an authorized account; sharing a link does not change their audience.
- Use **Edit** above your publication’s title. **Delete** and **Unpublish** are in **More editor options** for articles and at the bottom of the editor for notes. Editing loads the server's original source, autosaves unpublished changes, and updates the same post with its link and audience preserved. Deletion requires confirmation.
- **Unpublish → Move to drafts** saves the current edit locally before deleting the live publication. A failed draft save prevents deletion. If deletion fails, the draft stays available and you can retry. Republishing creates a new post and link; likes and replies do not transfer.
- Use **A−** and **A+** beside the book icon at the top of a publication to adjust its reading font size, including in focus mode. Your choice is saved on this device.
- Light, dark, and system themes; responsive layout; reading focus mode and full-window **Zen mode** for articles on desktop, available under **More editor options**. Keyboard shortcuts also work. On phones, tapping the article body automatically opens a distraction-free writing view sized to the visible keyboard area. Tap **Done** or dismiss the keyboard to return to the title and publication controls. Bottom navigation hides while typing.
- Article actions fit in one row: **Save draft**, **Preview**, **Publish**, and **More editor options**. **Schedule** is in the More menu. Formatting stays in one horizontally scrollable row; swipe it to reach more tools without giving up writing space.
- **More editor options → Smart punctuation** controls keyboard quotes, capitalization, and corrections. It starts off and remembers your choice. Even when enabled for prose, corrections turn off inside fenced, indented, and inline code. Existing writing, pasted content, and input-method composition are preserved.
- **More editor options → Vim mode** enables Vim keys only in the article body and remembers your choice. A small toolbar indicator shows Normal, Insert, Visual, or Visual line mode. Use `hjkl` (or arrow keys), `w/b/e`, `0/^/$`, and `gg/G` to move; `i/a/I/A` to insert; `o/O` to open a line; `v/V` to select characters or whole lines; and `Esc` to return to Normal without leaving Zen mode. Use `d/c/y` on a selection, `dd/cc/yy` on a line, `x` to delete a character, and `p/P` to paste the internal yank register. `u` or Cmd/Ctrl+Z uses browser undo; browser redo, copy/paste, save, and formatting shortcuts still work. In Normal/Visual mode, Tab leaves the body; Insert mode keeps Markdown indentation and list continuation. This is a small Vim subset: counts, arbitrary operator motions, search, macros, and Ex commands are not implemented. Titles, subtitles, notes, replies, and messages always use ordinary typing.
- Select a URL and tap **Link** to fill the URL field, then optionally enter display text. Select ordinary words to use them as the link text instead. Selecting a complete Markdown link lets you edit its destination and label.
- **Note** opens a compact social composer with your avatar, a text box, audience/content-warning settings, **Save draft**, and a **Note** button. There is no article title, subtitle, toolbar, or preview step. **Write an article** opens the full Markdown editor. Both kinds of draft resume from **Your writing**, and switching between them preserves the current draft.
- Both notes and articles have **Add images**: choose multiple files, paste a screenshot, or drop images into the composer. Uploads go to your connected server and respect its attachment, format, and file-size limits. Preview each image, add an accessible description (alt text), or remove it. Notes can contain only images. Processing and upload errors appear beside the attachments; **Check image** retries processing without uploading the file again.
- In articles, choose **Insert into article** to place an uploaded image at the cursor. **From URL** inserts an externally hosted HTTPS image. Images placed in the body are shown once in Fedistack; remaining attachments appear in a gallery below the text. Other fediverse clients may also display inline images as attachments. Existing attachments survive editing, and publishing or scheduling includes their IDs and descriptions.
- Markdown titles, subtitles, H2/H3 headings, bold, italic, strikethrough, bulleted/numbered/nested lists, checklists, quotes, link and image dialogs, inline code, code blocks, dividers, and reading preview. Formatting applies to selected lines; Enter continues lists and quotes, and Enter on an empty item ends them. Tab indents and Shift+Tab unindents selected lines. The **?** toolbar button opens a quick guide. This remains a small Markdown subset, not a full CommonMark implementation.
- Code blocks are highlighted in reading views and previews in both themes. Add a language after the opening triple backticks, such as `ts`, `js`, `python`, `c`, or `bash`. Unlabeled blocks use automatic detection; use `text` for plain code. Unsupported languages remain plain text. Highlighting works offline.
- Drafts autosave locally after 300 ms, then sync pending changes to the connected server every 30 seconds while writing. Local saves flush when leaving the editor or hiding the page. Drafts are scoped to server and account. Opening **Your writing** retrieves server drafts and changes from other clients. Continue, delete, and export drafts there. Returning online retries local drafts. A closed browser cannot finish a pending upload: wait for **Saved on server** before switching devices.
- **Your writing → Replies** lists your replies and comments, with links back to their conversations and controls to delete your own comments. Load older replies to browse your history. **Published** lists your articles and notes separately.
- Use **Save draft** or **Cmd/Ctrl+S** to save immediately without publishing or leaving the editor. A short **Saved** message confirms the local save; autosave continues in the background. Server sync status and errors appear in **Your writing**. Failed server saves retain the local copy. Changes made offline are preserved as a separate draft when they conflict with a newer server version.
- Leaving an article or note with work in progress opens a confirmation on desktop and mobile, including when using Back or starting another draft. Choose **Save draft & leave** to save and continue, or **Keep editing** to stay. A failed save keeps the editor open with retry and export options. **Your writing** shows a **Work in progress** card with **Continue editing** above every tab, so you can return to your latest draft even from Published. Edits to an existing publication remain unpublished until you choose **Update**.
- **Schedule** in the article or note editor accepts a date and time in your browser’s time zone, at least five minutes ahead. The server publishes automatically even with Fedistack closed. **Your writing → Scheduled** lists scheduled posts from all clients and lets you change their publication time or cancel them. Scheduling applies to new posts, not updates to existing publications.
- Publication counts, followers, following, likes, replies, word counts, and estimated reading times. Engagement totals explicitly cover loaded publications. No fabricated views or impressions.
- Separate DM conversations with private-mention replies. Context messages are checked against the exact participant set. A mention cannot silently add someone to a conversation. DMs are not end-to-end encrypted.

Server drafts use Mastodon’s scheduled-status API with dates spread across year **9999**. There is no documented indefinite draft mode: omitting `scheduled_at` when creating a status publishes immediately. Year 9999 is a finite workaround, not a guarantee of “never.” A server rejecting that date leaves the draft local and reports a sync error; Fedistack never retries it as an immediate post.

Other clients with scheduled-post support can see these copies, containing readable Markdown and a Fedistack metadata comment for restoring titles, subtitles, and editing state. Use Fedistack’s **Schedule** or **Publish** to remove that metadata and apply the server’s publication format; directly rescheduling a draft in another client may publish the raw text and metadata. Ordinary scheduled posts are kept separate from server drafts. Server character limits include draft metadata, and server scheduling quotas also apply. Export Markdown for an independent backup.

Drafts and server sync retain uploaded image references and descriptions. Markdown exports include image URLs, including attachments that were not inserted into the article; they do not bundle image files. Keep the original files: your server controls retention of unpublished uploads, and may expire them before an old draft is published. Removing an attachment from a draft removes its inline occurrences, without deleting the file from your server.

Offline demo drafts remain device-local. Demo data is fictional and kept separately from live account data. Remote messages are kept in memory rather than persisted in local storage. Conversations refresh when opened; timelines also reuse recent cached pages as described above. While a feed or profile is visible near the top, Fedistack checks for new content every 30 seconds (every 5 seconds for empty views). Updates pause in hidden tabs, while offline, while loading, or when scrolled down; failures back off to at most one attempt per two minutes. Discover keeps its loaded stream stable until you choose **Refresh** or revisit it from another tab. Failed requests have a **Try again** action; automatic loading pauses after errors or a batch with no matching posts, while manual loading remains available when older pages exist. There is no background streaming or push service.

Search covers content known to your connected server, not the whole fediverse. Enter `#topic` to read its hashtag timeline (with older pages); keyword searches show people, posts, and clickable hashtag suggestions, with **Load more results** for subsequent pages. Keyword search availability depends on your server. Full `@user@server` addresses and post URLs can resolve specific remote objects. Following remote people brings their future posts to your server; following a hashtag filters posts your server receives and does not subscribe to every server carrying that tag. Language, content-type, and Following only filters still apply to displayed posts.
